Part 1: Rust on Iron (Fe2O3·nH2O)

Remaining Pure Fe
100%
4Fe(s) + 3O2(g) + 2nH2O(l) → 2Fe2O3·nH2O(s) Reddish-brown rust
Live Observation (Iron on Ceiling): O2 and H2O move freely in the air, seep through the porous rust to attack Fe, and loose rust drops to the ground due to gravity!
  • Permeable & Porous: Rust has pores and cracks that allow air (O2) and water (H2O) in the air to pass through to inner Fe.
  • Loosely Bound & Drops Off: Rust falls to the ground under gravity, exposing a new surface of iron to rust more seriously!
🛡️ FURTHER PROTECTED (Protective)

Part 2: Aluminium Oxide (Al2O3)

Remaining Pure Al
100%
4Al(s) + 3O2(g) → 2Al2O3(s) Thin Natural Oxide
Live Observation: When Al is exposed to air, a continuous layer of Al2O3 quickly forms and blocks all O2 & H2O.
  • Impermeable: Non-porous Al2O3 blocks both air (O2) and water (H2O) from reaching the Al underneath.
  • Hard & Closely Bound: Firmly adheres to the metal surface and does NOT flake off, protecting Al from further corrosion.

Rust (hydrated iron(III) oxide) Aluminium oxide
Nature Fe2O3 · nH2O Al2O3
Chemical Equation(s)
Overall equation of rusting:
4Fe(s) + 3O2(g) + 2nH2O(l) → 2Fe2O3·nH2O(s)
Oxidation of Al:
4Al(s) + 3O2(g) → 2Al2O3(s)
Thickening of the oxide layer (Anodization) :
2Al(s) + 3H2O(l) → Al2O3(s) + 3H2(g)
Permeability permeable to both air and water impermeable to both air and water
Porous or not porous and loosely bound to the metal hard, closely bound to the metal
Protection
Not protected
  • This layer is permeable to air and water as it is porous.
  • The iron(III) oxide layer flakes off easily.
Further protected
  • This layer is impermeable to air and water.
  • The aluminium oxide layer firmly adheres to its surface.
Result ∴ New surface of iron then exposes to air and water and rusts more seriously. ∴ Can protect the aluminium underneath from further corrosion.
